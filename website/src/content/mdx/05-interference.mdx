## The Analogy

A traffic engineer can time traffic signals so that a green wave flows through a city — cars that hit one green light arrive at the next one green too. The same road network, with different *timing*, either **amplifies** flow or brings it to a halt.

Quantum interference works by the same logic: multiple amplitude paths combine, and their timing (phase) determines whether they **reinforce** or **cancel**.

## Constructive vs Destructive Interference

When two amplitude paths share the same phase, they reinforce:

$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{2} \quad \text{(constructive)}
$$

When they are opposite in phase, they cancel:

$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = 0 \quad \text{(destructive)}
$$

## The H–Z–H Sequence

Start from $|0\rangle$. Apply $H$ to get equal superposition. Apply $Z$ to flip the phase of $|1\rangle$:

$$
\frac{|0\rangle + |1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}} \xrightarrow{Z} \frac{|0\rangle - |1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}
$$

Apply $H$ again. The amplitudes now **interfere** and collapse entirely into $|1\rangle$:

$$
H\left(\frac{|0\rangle - |1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}\right) = |1\rangle
$$

The result is 100% $|1\rangle$ — a *certain* outcome engineered entirely through interference.

## Why This Matters for Algorithms

All major quantum speedups (Grover, Deutsch-Jozsa, QFT-based algorithms) work by designing a circuit whose interference **amplifies** the probability of correct answers while **suppressing** incorrect ones.

## Try It: Witness Interference

Apply `H → Z → H` in sequence. After all three gates, the probability of $|1\rangle$ should be 100%. This is not a coincidence — it is **constructive interference** at work. Remove the `Z` and run `H → H` instead. The qubit returns to $|0\rangle$ — destructive interference on $|1\rangle$.
